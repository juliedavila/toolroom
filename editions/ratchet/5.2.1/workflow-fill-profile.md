# Fill the profile

Answer what can be answered, mark what cannot, and let readiness say what matters next.

1. Take the questions from the interview table in `R1-rules-and-readiness.md`. Each row names the profile section, the questions, where the answer usually lives, and who must confirm it.
2. Answer from the organization's own sources first: service catalog, access governance, CI/CD and source control, change management, incident records, business impact and continuity plans, data classification.
3. Leave unknown answers as `FILL`. An unanswered field never refuses a profile; a false answer to a safety question does.
4. Record who confirmed each answer that needs confirmation, and when.
5. Run the profile checker in progressive mode and the readiness tool. Report three things to the person you work with: the highest phase the answers support, the questions that block the next phase (with where each answer lives and who confirms it), and any should-never rule an answer would break, with that rule's trade-off card from `R1`.
6. Name the security risk owner early: put the role in `separation.security_risk_owner_role` and record the accountable person's identity and link to that role in `org/decision-log.md` (`workflow-start.md`). Until that is answered, nobody can accept a patch or the overlay baseline. Name the overlay in `cloud_overlay.provider`: `aws` for the shipped example, or the organization's own overlay name.
7. Rename a team only on purpose. Risk sections record team names, so a rename makes every patch whose section names that team stale, and an accepted one goes back through acceptance.
8. Never record an exception's acceptance yourself. Draft the entry (the rule, the reason, candidate compensating controls, the roles that should discuss the card), and leave the acceptance fields for the named risk owners.

9. Three settings ship with no number: each owning team's fix cap (`fix_capacity.team_caps`, and the sponsor's `default_cap`), the injection policy's limits (`injection.stop_restore_max_minutes`, `production_max_exposure_minutes`) and the kept-test lifecycle dials (the `kept_test_` dials in `dials`). Derive each starting value from the organization's own records as the worked examples in part 3 show (`03-in-practice.md`, "Three settings ship with no number"): name the inputs and their source, show the arithmetic, record who agreed the result, and name the signal that will move it. Never copy an example's number into a profile. For a fix cap, put the window and the throughput in the same unit before multiplying: the remediation window is in days and the throughput is per month, so divide the window's days by 30 to get months. Use the window `fix_capacity.remediation_window_days_by_tier` sets for the consequence tier the team's findings mostly reach, the shortest of those windows when its findings are spread across tiers, and `remediation_window_days` when no tier has a window. Round the result down.

For a phase 1 rule-4 allowance, a worked separation assignment is `producer_team: Team Red`, `approver_team: Team Approvals`, `fixture_oracle_owner_team: Team Verify`, `verifier_team: Team Verify`, `fix_author_team: Team Alpha`, and `closure_acceptor_team: Team Closure`. The shared fixture and verifier team is allowed only within phase 1's exception; keep both separate from the producer and retain every other required separation. Record the security risk owner's rule_4_review_on in the phase plan, and arrange full separation or an accepted standard exception before that bound or phase 1 exit. Replace these illustrative teams with actual assignments.

Repeat as answers arrive. The phase 1 sections are few on purpose: who proposes, approves, holds the proof condition, verifies, fixes and accepts; the tools; the model routes; one protected asset; how the organization deploys; and the phase 1 boundary.
